Method, scope and verification

How this list was built, what counts as an official source, and what was deliberately left out.

Scope

  • City of San Francisco only. Events physically inside the San Francisco city limits are included. Events in other Bay Area cities are excluded, even when they are called "San Francisco" events (for example the San Francisco 49ers, who play every home game at Levi's Stadium in Santa Clara).
  • Two organising groups, as requested: events that occur on a regular schedule (every week / every month) are on the Regularly recurring page; events that recur year after year are on the Annual page; genuinely one-off dated events are on the Special page.
  • Category pages for civic meetings, arts and culture, sports, and markets and food are provided for usability. Every event appears on its category page and on the page matching its type, so the same row is never duplicated or contradicted.

Sourcing rules

  • Official sources only. Every row links to the organiser's own site, a City and County of San Francisco agency page, a state agency page, or a federally operated national-park page. Each link was opened and read on 2026-10-18.
  • No inference. If the official page did not state a value, the cell is left blank and marked not confirmed. Nothing has been filled in from prior knowledge, from a news article, or from an aggregator site.
  • No aggregators. Event-listing and tourism sites were used only to find the official page; they are never cited as the source of a value.
  • Disagreements are published, not resolved silently. Where two official pages disagree (for example two different meeting times for the same Board committee), the disagreement is recorded on the Flagged for review page and the affected cell is left blank.
  • Ended and cancelled events are shown. Events that have been discontinued by their organiser are listed on the Flagged for review page rather than silently omitted, so the gap is visible.

How the data is stored and built

  • data/events.py holds the canonical dataset; data/events.json is the machine-readable copy emitted by the build.
  • scripts/build_site.py renders the static HTML pages. There is no client-side JavaScript: every page is plain HTML that GitHub Pages can serve directly.
  • Re-running python3 scripts/build_site.py regenerates the site deterministically from the dataset, so the tables and the counts can never drift apart.

Known limitations

  • Individual performance dates for large seasons (opera, ballet, theatre) change and are sometimes revised late; the rows record the date ranges published on the official site at the time of checking.
  • Neighbourhood street fairs publish their next edition a few months ahead; the dates shown are the most recent confirmed editions, and later editions were not assumed.
  • Pool and court schedules change with the season; each row points at the department's own schedule page rather than restating a fixed timetable.
  • Where a commission does not publish a regular cadence, the frequency is blank rather than guessed from a handful of observed dates.
